# RegisterRAR: a Lean 4 check of *The Radial Acceleration Relation from the Granularity of Hilbert Space* (v0-18)

The paper: [all versions](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23226385), which always resolves to the latest version on Zenodo.

This project machine-checks the mathematics of the paper: the count of the register from Postulate 2, Einstein's equations and the de Sitter horizon from that count and thermodynamics, a string's energy from Palmer's relation, Newton's law as an equal share of a body's energy among the strings of a sphere, the share held only by the strings that carry the body's information, the scale `a₀`, the law and its two limits, the force between two bodies in the deep regime, the scale and `Λ` at every epoch in terms of `G`, and every number the paper quotes. The postulates and the physical inputs are never axioms here. Each enters as a structure or as a named hypothesis in the statement of the theorem that uses it, so a theorem's statement is its own ledger. For all 80 main theorems, `#print axioms` returns nothing beyond the logical minimum of classical mathematics, `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. There is no `sorry` and no `native_decide`.

The paper version checked is v0-18; each Lean file names it in its header. Build: Lean `v4.35.0-rc2`, Mathlib commit `045acef0f761280401116e3801cb78ed1b2e716b`, the versions of RegisterGravity and RegisterDimension. With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed, run `lake exe cache get` in this folder, then `lake build`; `lake env lean RegisterRAR/Ledger.lean > ledger_output.txt` prints the ledger and records it. The workflow in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` does both on GitHub after every change, and it fails on any warning and on any main theorem that depends on an axiom beyond the three.

The count, the positions along the rings, the model, and part of the chain of Sec. II come from RegisterGravity v11.2, where they compiled at the same versions; their proofs are unchanged.

## What each file proves

`Horizon.lean` formalizes the count of Sec. II from Postulate 2. The structure `Horizon L` holds exactly the clauses the count uses: every cell has one opposite, which lies on every largest ring through it; two cells that are not opposite lie on exactly one largest ring; any two largest rings share a cell; no ring holds every cell; and every largest ring has `L` cells. The paper's construction of a largest ring that misses a given cell (`exists_loop_not_mem`) gives `L/2` largest rings through every cell (`two_mul_card_through`), so the register has `L²/2 − L + 2` cells and `L²/4 − L/2 + 1` largest rings (`counts`), and `L` is even (`even_L`).

`Positions.lean` gives the largest rings their cyclic order, one cell per tick (Postulate 1), with each cell's opposite halfway round (Postulate 2), and gives every cell a distance from the observer's cell along the one largest ring through both. The two cells of an opposite pair lie at the distances `d` and `L/2 − d` (`dist_opp`), so every largest ring has half its cells inside the horizon a quarter of a ring away (`horizon_weight_ring`): the horizon counts every largest ring. At each distance below `L/2` there are exactly `L` cells (`card_dist_eq`), and the opposite lying halfway round makes `L` even (`even_of_halfway`).

`Models.lean` shows that these axioms have a model, the Fano plane with each point doubled into a cell and its opposite, at `L = 6` (`fano_counts`, `fano_even`), so that no theorem of the two files holds vacuously.

`Chain.lean` carries the rest of Sec. II over a structure `Reg` of positive constants: `c`, `ℏ`, `k_B`, the cell `ℓ_c`, `L`, `π` and `ln 2`, with `ln 2 < 1`. A string's two senses weighted equally hold one bit (`one_bit`). The temperature of a horizon from its period gives Unruh's temperature (`T_Unruh`) and, for the largest ring's lap, the Gibbons–Hawking one, `k_BT_dS = ℏc/2πR_Λ` (`T_dS`). Jacobson's coefficient of Einstein's equations is `8πG/c⁴` exactly when `G = c³k_B/4ℏη` (`einstein_coefficient`); the count fixes `η = πk_B ln 2/4ℓ_c²` (`eta_eq`), so `G = c³ℓ_c²/πℏ ln 2` (`G_eq`), the measured `G` fixes the cell at `ℓ_c = √(π ln 2) ℓ_P` (`cell_from_G`), and a horizon's entropy is a quarter of its area in Planck units (`entropy_quarter`). A largest ring has the length of a closed geodesic of the solution without a mass, so the horizon's radius is `R_Λ = Lℓ_c/2π = √(3/Λ)`, and that solution has its horizon there (`radius_from_geodesics`, `deSitter`); the paper excludes the quotients of de Sitter space that keep the symmetry of space, one by the direction of time of Postulate 1's shift and one by Postulate 2's one opposite. With the Komar energy, Newton's law is an equal share of a body's energy among the strings of a sphere, two Landauer energies each (`newton_komar`, `newton_share`, `Ns_eq`). The vacuum energy inside the horizon, `c⁴R_Λ/2G`, is the temperature times the horizon's entropy, one Landauer heat per string (`vacuum_per_string`). Palmer's relation, with a free string's reduction time one lap of a largest ring, gives a string the energy `k_BT_dS` (`string_energy`), and that energy splits into the vacuum's heat, `ln 2` of it, and matter, `1 − ln 2` (`string_split`).

`DeSitter.lean` proves that the horizon of the solution without a mass lies a quarter of a largest ring from the observer, at the proper distance `πR_Λ/2 = Lℓ_c/4` (`proper_distance`, with the arcsine `proper_distance_exists`), and that its period is the time light takes to go once around a largest ring (`horizon_period`), so that its temperature is the Gibbons–Hawking one (`horizon_temperature`).

`Law.lean` formalizes Secs. III–IX. A body's energy is held only by the strings that carry its information, shared equally among them (`share_of_equipartition`); with a fraction `b` of carriers the acceleration is `g_N/b` (`share_acceleration`). The body's energy per string, in units of a string's matter, is `f = g_N/a₀` with `a₀ = (1 − ln 2)c²/(2 ln 2 R_Λ)` (`fill_fraction`). With the threshold exponential with mean one, `b = 1 − e^{−√f}` and `a = g_N/(1 − e^{−√(g_N/a₀)})` (`law`); a threshold of mean `μ` would give the scale `μ²a₀` (`law_scale_mean`). Near the body `a = g_N(1 + e^{−h}) + g_N e^{−2h}/(1 − e^{−h})` (`near_limit`); far from it, for `h = √(g_N/a₀) ≤ 1/2`, `a` lies within `a₀h³` of `√(a₀g_N) + g_N/2` (`deep_limit`); on a circular orbit in the deep regime `v⁴ = a₀GM` (`tully_fisher`); and a classical share, the probability `b = f` in place of the amplitude, would give `a = a₀` and rotation speeds rising as `√r` (`classical_limit`), so flat rotation curves support Postulate 1. With Jacobson's `G`, the scale is a fixed fraction of the Planck acceleration, `a₀ = (1 − ln 2)√π/((ln 2)^{3/2}L) · √(c⁷/ℏG)`, and `Λ = 12πc³/(L²ℏG ln 2)`; the same formula with another value `G'` of Newton's constant gives twice the scale only if `G' = G/4` (`scale_over_time`, Eq. (16)). For two bodies (Sec. VII): on a sphere the body's share at a string, `Mc²/N_s`, equals the carrier fraction times what a carrier holds, `b·2k_BT_a ln 2` (`share_reading`), so Eq. (10) is unchanged when the share is read from the acceleration at the patch, the reading that conserves momentum between two bodies; momentum conservation, `M₁a₁ = M₂a₂`, gives each body the acceleration `F/M_i` (`two_body_split`); and Milgrom's virial theorem for the deep regime, Eq. (14), gives a force `F/M₂` that lies between `√(GM₁a₀)/r − (2/3)√(Ga₀M₂)/r` and `√(G(M₁ + M₂)a₀)/r`, so that it tends to Eq. (11) as `M₂ → 0` (`two_body_deep`), and that equals `(4/3)(√2 − 1) M√(GMa₀)/r` for equal masses (`two_body_equal`).

`Relation.lean` chains Secs. II–VI into one theorem, `radial_acceleration_relation`, whose hypotheses are exactly the inputs the paper claims and whose conclusion is the law with the derived scale, Newton's share, and a string's share of two Landauer energies at its temperature, `κ = 2k_B ln 2`. Eq. (4) is derived inside it from Postulate 1 and equipartition, with Eq. (3). `hypotheses_consistent` shows that these hypotheses can all hold at once, for every register.

`Numerics.lean` recomputes every number the paper quotes from the inputs of Appendix A, as exact rationals with Mathlib's `π`, `ln 2` and `e`, and certifies each by an interval every point of which rounds to the quoted value: `H_∞`, `R_Λ` and `c²/R_Λ` (`horizon_values`), the coefficient `0.2213` (`coefficient`), `a₀ = 1.200 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻²` (`a0_val`) and its uncertainty `0.011` (`a0_uncertainty`), its distance from the measurements (`a0_sigmas`) and with the local `H₀` (`a0_local_H0`), the threshold's mean read from the data (`threshold_mean_from_data`), the companion's bound `πc²/12R_Λ` (`elastic_bound`), the horizon's thermal energy, temperature and a string's matter (`thermal_values`), the energy per string that Planck's `Ω_Λ` implies (`string_energy_check`), the predicted vacuum share `ln 2` against Planck and DESI (`vacuum_share`), the coefficients in units of `cH₀` (`cH0_coefficients`), the Tully–Fisher speeds (`tully_fisher_speeds`), where the deep regime begins (`deep_radii`), the solar system (`solar_system`), the departure at Neptune, `3 × 10⁻¹⁰²` (`neptune_departure`), and `a/g_N` at four accelerations (`rar_table`). It also builds the register of the measured constants and shows that its scale is the certified one (`reg_a0`) and that the measured `G` meets Jacobson's relation for it (`reg_jacobson`).

## What was checked, and what it found

- v0-16 built with `lake build --wfail` without a single warning ("Build completed successfully (2278 jobs)"), and its ledger reported all 74 listed theorems, each depending on `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` and nothing else. v0-17 adds `Reg.string_energy` and `Numerics.vacuum_share`, so its ledger lists 76 theorems, and replaces the hypothesis `hεs` of the main theorem by `hDP` and `hClock`; it adds no file and no import.
- v0-18 replaces the hypothesis `hK` of the main theorem by `hcar`, `hNcar`, `hκ` and `hκN`, from which the theorem derives `κ = 2k_B ln 2` and Eq. (4), and adds `Reg.share_reading`, `Law.two_body_split`, `Law.two_body_deep` and `Law.two_body_equal` for Sec. VII, so its ledger lists 80 theorems. It adds no file and no import. v0-18 built with `lake build --wfail` without a single warning ("Build completed successfully (2278 jobs)"), with Mathlib compiled from source at `045acef`, and its ledger, shipped as `ledger_output.txt`, reports all 80 listed theorems, each depending on `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` and nothing else.
- The statement of `Relation.radial_acceleration_relation`, printed at the top of the ledger, lists the paper's claimed inputs and no others. Its hypotheses can all hold at once, for every register (`Relation.hypotheses_consistent`), and the measured `G` meets Jacobson's relation for the register of the measured constants (`Numerics.reg_jacobson`), so the theorem does not hold vacuously. The combinatorial clauses of the postulates have a model (`Models.lean`).

- Eq. (13) of v0-9 gave `a₀ = 0.2214 c²/R_Λ`. The coefficient `(1 − ln 2)/2 ln 2` is `0.22135`, which is `0.2213` to four places (`Numerics.coefficient`); the paper says `0.2213` since v0-10.
- Appendix A of v0-10 quoted `a/g_N = 1.044, 1.582, 3.689, 10.51`. These are correct, but certifying `3.689` to four figures needs a sharper bound on `exp` than the two used here (`1 + x ≤ eˣ`, and `eˣ < 1/(1 − x)` for `0 < x < 1`). Since v0-11 the paper quotes three figures, `1.04, 1.58, 3.69, 10.5`, which `Numerics.rar_table` certifies.
- Eq. (11) gives the deep regime as `a = √(a₀g_N) + g_N/2 + …`. `Law.deep_limit` certifies it with an explicit remainder: for `h = √(g_N/a₀) ≤ 1/2`, `a` lies within `a₀h³ = g_N^{3/2}/√a₀` of `√(a₀g_N) + g_N/2`.

## What is not certified

- Palmer's relation and the reading of a free string's reduction time as the lap of a largest ring enter as `hDP` and `hClock`; Lean checks what follows from them, `ε_s = k_BT_dS`, not the relation itself.
- The physical inputs of the table above are hypotheses, not theorems. Lean checks that the paper's conclusions follow from them, and from nothing else; it does not check the inputs.
- Jacobson's derivation itself, the Raychaudhuri equation and the Clausius relation on every local horizon, enters as `hJ`; Lean checks the algebra of its coefficient (`Reg.einstein_coefficient`).
- That the exponential maximizes entropy for a positive quantity of given mean enters as `hME`; a proof would need measure theory.
- The classification of the maximally symmetric vacua and their closed geodesics enters as `hGeo`; Lean checks what follows from it, the radius `R_Λ = Lℓ_c/2π` and the horizon there.
- Sec. VII's field equation, `∇·(b∇Φ) = 4πGρ`, the net force on an isolated system, `−(1/8πG)∫|∇Φ|²∇b dV`, and Milgrom's virial theorem are vector calculus; they enter as `hvir`, and Lean checks what follows: the split of the force (`two_body_split`), its test-body limit and its equal-mass value (`two_body_deep`, `two_body_equal`), and that the two readings of a string's share agree on a sphere (`share_reading`).
- Sec. VIII's comparison with the simple interpolating function of Milgrom's phenomenology, a difference of at most 5% over the measured range, and Fig. 2 are numerical illustrations and are not certified.
- Lean has no epochs. Sec. IX applies Eq. (16) at every epoch with that epoch's `G`, since `L` and the split of a string's energy do not change and our units fix `c` and `ℏ`; `Reg.scale_over_time` checks the formula and what a doubled scale requires of `G`. The measured bounds on a change of `G` that Sec. IX cites are quoted, not computed.
- The docstrings tie the Lean objects to the paper's strings, qubits and bodies; Lean checks the mathematics, not that physical interpretation.
